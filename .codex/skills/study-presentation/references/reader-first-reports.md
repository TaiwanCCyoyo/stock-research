# Reader-first reports

Use when designing a study's reading path or repairing a report that is accurate
but hard to understand. Sources consulted on 2026-10-31. These are adaptations
for saved-result research reports, not a requirement to turn web pages into slides.

## Make the finding the organizing unit

[Harvard Catalyst's assertion-evidence guidance](https://catalyst.harvard.edu/writing-communication-center/visualize-science/slides/)
uses a clear message headline supported by visual evidence. Apply this to a
report panel: a title such as "主要比較" identifies a category but gives no answer.
Write what the comparison shows, then support it with an appropriate chart,
timeline or labelled values. Keep observational claims observational.

Unlike a spoken presentation, a report must supply its own context. Do not copy
slide word limits or remove definitions merely to produce a sparse page.

## Write the answer before the analysis

[ONS guidance on main points](https://service-manual.ons.gov.uk/content/writing-for-users/writing-main-points-and-analysis)
starts with the most useful finding and keeps supporting analysis in the same
logical order. Before implementing the UI, write the question, answer and
supporting comparison in plain language. Avoid making the reader derive the
answer from counts of tests, output files or comparison cells. Each section
should advance that answer rather than repeat the numbers already visible.

## Keep the required context within view

[NN/g's recognition-over-recall principle](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/recognition-and-recall/)
favors visible cues over remembered information. Research codes and invented
group names should not be the only labels on a selector or chart. Keep the
meaningful condition nearby. Mark the anchor, signal-observation window and
outcome window spatially when chronology determines the comparison. A caption
must identify whose percentage is shown; changing the denominator changes the
question even when both values use percent signs.

## Defer detail without hiding the meaning

[NN/g's progressive-disclosure guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)
keeps the primary task visible and makes secondary material easy to reach.
For this project, expose a useful default comparison, then its selected evidence;
put complete grids and technical definitions in clearly named secondary views.
Do not hide the population, time origin or decisive limitation in a tooltip.
Opening one comparison must not force the reader through the entire grid.

## Explain uncertainty where it changes the answer

[Government Analysis Function guidance](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/communicating-quality-uncertainty-and-change/)
places important quality limitations alongside results and explains their
effect on interpretation. State a concrete boundary, such as a missing independent
period, instead of repeating a general instruction not to trade. Preserve
unknowns and the distinction between association and causation. The source is
being updated for accessibility; use it for communication principles, not as a
current accessibility specification.

## Verify understanding separately from factual correctness

The project's independent-review requirement is an editorial process, not a
claim that the sources above validate AI reviewers. First test whether a fresh
reader can explain the overview without author hints. Then check that explanation
against the mission and saved evidence. Passing arithmetic or source checks does
not establish that readers understand the wording, layout or interaction; actual
reader feedback and desktop/mobile acceptance remain necessary.
